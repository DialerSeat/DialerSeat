# YouTube plan: own "cold calling" on YouTube, sell the seat underneath

Built 2026-10-13 from a demand map of YouTube itself, using the method in the
brief (validated demand → packaging → promise/proof/plan → useful CTA →
data loop). Reproducible with `scripts/youtube/` (run `demand_map.py`, then
`channel_baselines.py`, then `rank_outliers.py`): 50 buyer search
phrases, 868 video results, 327 channels with a baseline of their recent
uploads, 207 videos at 3× or more their own channel's median ("outliers").

## 2. The channel

**One audience:** people whose income depends on how many conversations they
have on the phone. Every video has to be for them. An off-topic hit brings
viewers who ignore everything after it (brief, "your highest viewed video can
be your least valuable").

**What DialerSeat is on camera:** the tool in the video, never the topic.
Every dialing session is filmed on DialerSeat, so the product is on screen for
the whole video and is never pitched. That is what made the Instantly video
in the brief "the best ad of all time".

**The proof statement** (the "why listen to us" line, used in every hook):

> "We build a dialer, so we see every call: when people pick up, which
> openers get hung up on, which numbers get flagged Spam Likely."

It needs no personal name. The person on camera can be you, unnamed, or a
hired caller; the site and channel stay under the company name. Verify the
call count before quoting it (about 12,800 platform calls as of October 2026).

## 4. Packaging

- **Thumbnail first.** One result, readable in a glance: the number (10
  booked, 100 openers, 12,800 calls), the DialerSeat dialer screen with a
  live call timer, and at most three words in white on a red block. Clean and
  sharp, matching the editor brief; no AI imagery.
- **Title formats proven in this niche:** "speedrunning X (i show
  everything)", "watch me X in N hours", "I tried N X. These are the best.",
  "We analyzed N calls", "N years of X in N minutes", "Brutally honest advice
  about X in N minutes", "The new era of X has just begun", "If you don't
  understand X, you don't understand Y" (for example: if you don't understand
  the 3% rule, you don't understand predictive dialing).
- **Test before publishing:** mock the thumbnail and title beside the current
  top results for the same search and ask two people which they would click.

## 5. The first 30 seconds: promise, proof, plan

> **Promise:** "In the next 10 hours I'm going to book 10 appointments from
> expired listings, and you'll see every call."
> **Proof:** "We build a dialer, so we see every call made on it: when people
> pick up, what gets hung up on."
> **Plan:** "You'll get the list we start with, the opener, the three
> objections that come up every time, and the exact numbers at the end."

Live-call and speedrun videos are not scripted; only the hook and the
transitions are. Edit for clarity, not motion: a visual stays only if it
proves or explains something.

## 6. Calls to action that viewers thank you for

- The product appears because the video is filmed in it. Never a pitch
  segment.
- Each video offers one free thing the viewer wants next: the script, the
  opener list, the objection sheet, the connect-rate data, a calculator.
  Opt-ins for the downloadable sheets need a form (not built yet); the
  data pages and calculators exist today.
- **Every link carries the video's name**, so the admin view attributes
  visits and sign-ups to it. The tracker already reads these:
  `?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=video&utm_campaign=<video-slug>`
  (for Instagram clips: `utm_source=ig&utm_medium=reel&utm_campaign=<video-slug>`).

## 7. The loop

After every upload, and monthly for the whole channel, read the funnel:
**views → site visits → sign-ups → paid seats**, per `utm_campaign`, and fix
the narrowest step. A video with views and no visits needs a better CTA; a
video with visits and no sign-ups points at the landing page.

Double down on why a winner worked, not its title: if "speedrunning
insurance" wins, run the same mechanism for solar setters and expireds, not
the same video again. Re-run the research monthly; the market votes, the
research is only a hypothesis.

## 8. Legal care for live calls (read before filming)

- **Recording consent.** About a dozen US states (California, Florida,
  Illinois, Pennsylvania, Washington and others) require every party's
  consent to record a call. Get consent on the call, or publish only your
  side, or call only one-party-consent states, and take legal advice before
  publishing consumer calls.
- **No personal data on screen:** blur names, numbers and addresses.
- **The calls themselves must be lawful:** Do Not Call scrubbing, calling
  hours, and the abandoned-call limit in predictive. A compliance mistake on
  camera is a permanent exhibit.
- Clips of other creators (Glencoco's Hormozi clips, for example) need the
  rights; don't copy that part.

## 9. Cadence and roles

- One long video a week, plus 3 to 5 Instagram clips cut from it.
- Research: re-run `scripts/youtube/` monthly and compare the outlier list
  with last month's.
- Editor: clean cuts, captions, thumbnails to the rules above; no AI
  imagery or voices.
- You: the dialing sessions and the decisions on what to publish.
