## What hasn't been done that the book says matters most

1. **Links (chapters 23–29).** None yet: no emails sent, no launch, no directory profiles. The book's single most expensive mistake was starting this in month seven; DialerSeat is in month three. Everything needed is in [link-kit.md](link-kit.md).
2. **Search Console export (chapters 16, 17 and 23, Appendix D).** Without it there's no way to tell which pages are stuck at position 6–10 (needing links) from which have a title problem (needing a rewrite).
3. **The monthly AI question set (chapters 13 and 33).** Never run, so there's no starting point to compare against.
4. **The entity work (chapter 9).** Company profiles and `sameAs`, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, the www redirect, and the two spelling-variant domains.
5. **What happens after the click (chapters 18–19).** No dead-click or real-speed measurement.

## Double down: what to multiply

The book's chapter 3 says you can't predict winners, but once one shows up, put cheap attempts into the same template. Two templates have now shown up.

1. **More countries in calling rules by country.** It's 15 countries now and the cluster brings about 30% of Google visitors, with two signups. Each new country is about 12 pages (one per language). Candidates are countries with outbound call centres or offshore agents dialing the US: Pakistan, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Jamaica, the Dominican Republic, Honduras, Guatemala, El Salvador, Peru, Argentina, Ireland, New Zealand, Singapore, the UAE and Turkey. The book's rule (chapter 37): add a country only when its own rules can be read from a primary source, never as a page that only changes the country name.
2. **US state-by-state calling rules.** The same template for "telemarketing laws by state", which autocomplete confirms, as do "mini tcpa states" and "cold calling laws in [state]". The state Do Not Call table already reads eleven states from their own sites. Ship only states with real substance: registration, bond, mini-TCPA, calling hours or their own list.
3. **More translated tools.** Translated tool pages were found within days. Each new tool is twelve pages. The call screening script generator is already drafted.
4. **The dialer.io landing page.** It's the busiest page and converts one in fifteen. These visitors are looking at a dialer that won't publish a price and asks for three months; the page should make that comparison in its first screen. It's worth a read-through before any change, under the "don't rewrite pages at positions 6–10" rule, which this page may not fall under.
5. **The copy review.** Approving it fixes 23 titles and 98 descriptions in all twelve languages at once. Low on page one, a title that names the searcher's worry gets about eight times more clicks (chapter 7, inferred from search wording).

## Order I'd do it in

1. Links: start the outreach emails this week, three to five a day, and the Peerlist launch.
2. Search Console export, so the next decision is based on positions rather than guesses.
3. Five more countries in calling rules, from primary sources, then judge them in four to six weeks.
4. The copy review.
5. The call screening tool.
6. US state pages, starting with Florida, Texas and Oklahoma (mini-TCPA states).

**Don't judge** any of this before late January, and compare 28-day windows only.
