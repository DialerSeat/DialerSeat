Getting DialerSeat Recommended by AI Assistants

What it takes for ChatGPT and similar tools to name DialerSeat when somebody asks for a dialer · 23 August 2026

The short version. You have already built more of the technical groundwork than most companies ever do. The gap is not on your website — it is that nothing outside your website corroborates it. A model reads a vendor's own marketing, recognises it as a vendor's own marketing, and looks for independent confirmation before putting that name in front of a user.

Two mechanisms, only one of them winnable

Training data

The model absorbed information about you during pre-training. This is slow, uncontrollable, and cannot be bought. It rewards products that have been widely discussed for years. Treat it as a lagging consequence of everything else, never as a lever.

Retrieval

The model searches the web during the conversation, reads what it finds, and cites it. This is where essentially all winnable ground is, it responds in weeks rather than years, and it is what the llms.txt work already targets.

The critical thing to understand: being quotable and being recommendable are different problems. Your own pages make you quotable. Third-party sources make you recommendable. You have the first and almost none of the second.

What already exists

Built and working

  • canonicalFacts.ts — one source of truth rendering /llms.txt, /llms-full.txt and every markdown mirror, so retrieved pages cannot contradict each other on price or terms
  • Ten competitor comparison pages — ReadyMode, Mojo, PhoneBurner, Five9, Convoso, BatchDialer, WAVV, Kixie, Orum, JustCall
  • schema.ts — structured markup describing the product to machines
  • FAQ pages answering real questions in plain language
  • Pricing stated openly rather than hidden behind "contact sales"

That last point matters more than it looks. A model cannot recommend a product whose price it cannot state, and most of your competitors hide theirs.

What kind of reviews

Specificity beats volume, and recency beats both.

Ten reviews naming the situation — "final expense floor", "five-line predictive", "no contract, we scale seats weekly", "moved off ReadyMode" — outperform two hundred saying "great product, great support". Models match against the language of the question, and buyers do not ask "what is the best dialer". They ask:

  • "cheap dialer for a small insurance team"
  • "predictive dialer with no contract"
  • "ReadyMode alternative"
  • "dialer that bills weekly"

Your reviews need to contain that vocabulary or they will not be retrieved for it. Reviews stating checkable facts — the price, the billing terms, what it replaced, how many seats — are the most quotable. Adjectives are not.

Two cautions

Do not buy reviews

Platforms detect and delist them, and more importantly it poisons the exact signal being built. A model surfacing an obviously astroturfed review page is worse for you than absence, because absence is neutral and fakery is evidence.

Be sceptical of "AEO" and "LLM optimisation" services

Overwhelmingly repackaged SEO at a premium, sold on the novelty of the acronym. The technical half is the half you have already done yourself.

The strategic point

Models recommend narrow things confidently and broad things vaguely. DialerSeat will not become the answer to "best dialer" — Five9 and Convoso own that ground through sheer corpus volume built over a decade.

It can absolutely become the answer to "no-contract dialer for a small agency that bills weekly", because almost nobody occupies that ground and your pricing model is the differentiator rather than any feature. Predictive dialing, AMD, recordings and dispositions are table stakes; weekly billing with no seat minimum and no contract is not.

The comparison pages are your strongest existing asset for this. "X alternative" is the highest-intent query in the category, and you have ten of those pages already built and live.

Suggested order

  1. List on G2, Capterra, GetApp and Software Advice. Free, a few hours, largest single gap.
  2. Ask your two current paying customers for reviews, with a nudge toward describing their use case rather than rating you out of five.
  3. Publish demo video — transcripts become retrievable text.
  4. Participate honestly in the communities where your buyers already are, as an operator rather than as an advert.
  5. Keep the comparison pages current. They are already doing the work.
