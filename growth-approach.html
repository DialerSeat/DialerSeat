The Growth Approach

DialerSeat · 24 August 2026

The number that drives the whole plan. Seven strangers found DialerSeat on Google. Three of them dialled. Three of them paid. That is roughly 40% activation and 40% conversion from cold organic search — with no marketing, no sales calls, no trial, and a $35 barrier on day one. One of them, chrisharb1987, has put 2,167 calls through the platform.

Small sample, so treat it as a signal rather than a rate. But the signal points one way: the channel that is already working has never been fed.

The strategy in one line

Feed the channel that already converts before buying colder traffic. Someone typing "predictive dialer no contract" arrives pre-qualified — they have the problem and they are shopping. Someone receiving a cold DM has neither. The same 100 signups are worth wildly different amounts depending on where they came from.

Phase 0 — Unblock, before any traffic at all

Nothing below matters if the stack stops

  1. Vercel Pro. Hobby is non-commercial and you have been taking payment on it since early July. This is catch-up, not preparation.
  2. Supabase Pro. Free goes read-only at 500 MB — under a week of runway once a real floor dials — and the Micro instance lands at 40–70 predictive agents.
  3. Register the number pool. All 9 active numbers currently read is_registered: false. Unregistered numbers get flagged fastest, and answer rate is the only thing a trial user judges you on.
  4. One real dialing day at volume on the current build.

Phase 1 — Pour into organic search

The channel that produced every paying customer you have

Already built, and unusually well

canonicalFacts.ts feeding /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, ten competitor comparison pages, schema markup, FAQ pages, and pricing stated openly rather than hidden behind "contact sales" — which matters more than it looks, because a model cannot recommend a product whose price it cannot state.

  1. List on the review platforms. G2, Capterra, GetApp, Software Advice, TrustRadius. Free, a few hours, and the single biggest gap. These are scraped constantly and cited heavily, and they are structured exactly the way a model wants — category, price, rating, features.
  2. Ask chrisharb1987 and info@lsgrp.org for reviews. Nudge them toward describing the situation — "final expense floor", "no contract, we scale seats weekly", "moved off ReadyMode" — not toward rating you out of five. Ten specific reviews beat two hundred saying "great product".
  3. Extend the comparison pages. "X alternative" is the highest-intent query in the category and you already have ten of them live.
  4. Publish the edited video. Transcripts are indexed, so it becomes retrievable text as well as video — a second return on work you are doing anyway. Lead with the latency: the click and the "hello", split-screen. It is the one thing a viewer feels in three seconds and it cannot be faked.
  5. Participate in the communities where your buyers already are, as an operator rather than as an advert.

Phase 2 — Cut time to first ring

The biggest conversion lever, whatever the channel

  1. A prebuilt demo campaign. Right now a new user must create a campaign, upload and map a CSV, and understand dialer modes before hearing anything. Every one of those is a place to close the tab. Sample leads pointed at numbers you control would let somebody hear the 0.09-second connect before doing any setup at all — your differentiator, currently sitting behind twenty minutes of work.

Phase 3 — The trial, to unlock colder channels

Not a fix for the current funnel — an unlock for the next one

You convert cold strangers at ~40% while asking $35 up front, so price does not look like the objection — reach does. The trial's real job is making ads and DMs viable at all, since that audience will not pay on first contact.

  1. Card required. Kills serial re-signup, filters people who were never buying, and roughly doubles conversion because the default becomes "keep it".
  2. Card fingerprint check only. Stripe documents fingerprint for exactly this: "check whether two customers who've signed up with you are using the same card number". No blocking of prepaid, credit, or wallet cards — the convenience is worth more than the marginal abuse it would stop.
  3. Seats never trial, whatever the owner is doing. Done — trial_period_days: 0 is now explicit on seat subscriptions.
  4. Cap by usage as well as time. 7 days or N dials, whichever comes first. Bounds both the cost and the pool exposure, and is honest if stated upfront.
  5. One number pool, not two. A separate trial pool becomes the degraded pool, and trial users are exactly the audience whose answer rates you can least afford to be bad.

The failure mode to watch — and it is not a spam label

number-health cannot tell a bad number from a bad list. One trial user dialling a scraped file of dead numbers collapses the answer rate on every number they touch, and the health cron does exactly what it was built to do: rests them — pulling good numbers out of rotation for paying customers. No carrier decision involved. Watch for a cluster of rested_reason: low_answer_rate right after a trial signup, and use the WAKE button, which clears the health counters as well as the status.

Phase 4 — Then, and only then, paid and cold

  1. Ads and cold DMs, once the trial exists and activation is fast. That traffic converts far worse than search and needs both to work at all.

What not to do

Don'tWhy
Chase the four dead signupsZero campaigns, zero leads, zero calls. They never saw the product work. Lowest-yield hour available.
Buy reviewsPlatforms delist them, and it poisons the exact signal you are building. Absence is neutral; fakery is evidence.
Buy "AEO" or "LLM optimisation" servicesOverwhelmingly repackaged SEO. The technical half you have already done yourself.
Aim at "best dialer"Five9 and Convoso own that through a decade of corpus volume. "No-contract dialer for a small agency that bills weekly" is winnable and nearly unoccupied.
Publish a real prospect's call audioTwo-party consent is a legal problem, and an outbound dialer showing off a stranger's voice tells every buyer what happens to their leads.
